## Reading
- The overlap is in direction: lower tax on labour, weighted to the top; a smaller public sector; pension funds out of
  designated bonds into the capital market; fewer local authorities; privatisation.
- The government plan departs from his on the points he stressed most: it fires public employees and cuts nominal pay,
  both of which his document rejects, and it lets the deficit rise above 3%.
- The document cannot show that the government plan came from him. Several shared items (designated bonds,
  local-authority mergers, privatisation) were Treasury and Bank of Israel positions before 2003 [not checked here].
- Not checked: whether a Treasury draft of the plan existed before 1.3.2003; Netanyahu became finance minister when
  the government was sworn in at the end of February 2003.
